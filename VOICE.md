# Phil voice and editorial judgement

This document describes the authorial judgement hickinsons.blog is trying to preserve when AI is involved in drafting or editing.

It complements [EDITORIAL.md](EDITORIAL.md). `EDITORIAL.md` defines the publication standard and hard gates. This file is narrower: it describes how Phil tends to decide what sounds true, proportionate and worth saying.

It is not a style-transfer prompt and should not become one. Surface features such as short paragraphs, questions, headings or aphoristic lines are techniques, not the voice.

## Sources of authority

When signals conflict, prefer them in this order:

1. Phil's explicit source material, comments and edits for the piece being worked on.
2. Explicit author decisions recorded in reviewed pull requests or editorial issues.
3. Durable learnings in `docs/editorial-learnings/` that are relevant to the current material.
4. Published writing, read for judgement and range rather than copied for surface style.
5. Inference from recurring textual patterns.

Never invent a personal fact, reaction, opinion, memory or conclusion in order to make a draft sound more like Phil.

## The centre of the voice

### Start with what actually happened

Phil's strongest writing normally has something real underneath it: a work situation, a conversation, a trip, a book, a technical problem, a family moment or something he has noticed.

Stay with the concrete material long enough for the reader to see why it matters. Do not rush from an anecdote into a universal lesson simply because an essay appears to need one.

### Earn the abstraction

Phil is interested in systems, patterns and implications, but the abstraction should grow out of the evidence rather than arrive pre-packaged.

A useful draft often asks:

- what is actually happening here?
- what assumption is hiding inside the obvious explanation?
- what changes if the definition, incentive, technology or constraint changes?
- what remains unresolved after the neat answer has been removed?

Frameworks are welcome when the material genuinely needs one. Do not manufacture a framework from a small human observation.

### Prefer ordinary language to impressive language

Use British English and generally choose the word Phil might plausibly use in conversation over the word that makes the sentence sound more literary, academic or polished.

Technical language is useful when it adds precision. Abstract vocabulary is not a substitute for precision.

When Phil supplies a phrase, joke, analogy or unusual piece of wording, treat it as high-value evidence. Do not silently "improve" it into a smoother synonym unless meaning or readability requires it.

### Be thoughtful without performing thoughtfulness

The voice is curious, sceptical and comfortable with uncertainty, but it should not manufacture ambiguity merely to sound reflective.

It can reach a conclusion when the evidence supports one. It can also leave something open when it is genuinely open.

Avoid the familiar machinery of AI-reflective prose: repeated "not X, but Y" constructions, staged one-line reveals, artificial question ladders, grand summaries and endings that explain the meaning of a moment after the moment has already done the work.

### See the system without losing the people

Phil naturally notices structures, incentives, data, technology, capability, organisations and unintended consequences. The systems view should deepen the human observation rather than flatten it.

People are not examples inserted to prove a model. A relationship, colleague, family moment or ordinary exchange may be important precisely because it resists reduction to a framework.

### Implicate the author

The writing is strongest when Phil is willing to notice his own habits, contradictions and temptations rather than diagnosing everybody else from a distance.

That does not require confession. Often a small admission — wanting to take over, being unsure, getting something wrong, noticing an instinct — carries more authority than a general prescription.

### Use humour as recognition, not performance

Humour is usually understated and attached to something concrete. It works best when it sounds like an observation Phil would actually make rather than a crafted punchline.

Do not add jokes merely to make the prose feel more human.

### Stop when the point has landed

Do not assume every piece needs a final lesson, a title echo or a polished statement of significance.

The ending can return to a moment, acknowledge uncertainty, make a clear conclusion or simply stop. If the final paragraphs mainly explain what the reader has already understood, remove them and see whether the piece improves.

## What a Phil pass should look for

A Phil pass is not a synonym pass. Review the draft for judgement before polishing sentences.

Ask:

- **Provenance:** Is every personal claim grounded in something Phil actually supplied?
- **Proportion:** Has a small observation been made to carry more meaning than it can support?
- **Abstraction:** Did the draft leave the concrete material too early?
- **Vocabulary:** Are there words or phrases Phil would be unlikely to use himself?
- **Rhythm:** Has the prose fallen into repeated short-paragraph or reveal patterns?
- **Certainty:** Is the draft more certain, or more artificially uncertain, than Phil's evidence warrants?
- **Human detail:** Has the editor removed the odd, ordinary or funny detail that made the piece specific?
- **Ending:** Has the piece already finished before the final paragraph?

The goal is not to maximise a voice score. The goal is to reduce the amount of correction Phil has to make while preserving his authorship and judgement.

## Relationship to editorial learnings

`VOICE.md` should remain compact and relatively stable.

The richer evidence lives in `docs/editorial-learnings/`. A learning belongs there only when a real editorial cycle produced a non-obvious, durable and material lesson that future drafting could otherwise repeat or have to rediscover.

Do not promote every preference into this file. Promote a learning when it recurs across multiple pieces or Phil explicitly establishes it as a broad preference. Changes to this file require normal review; no automated workflow may silently rewrite the model of Phil's voice.
