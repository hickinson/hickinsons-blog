```text
At an internal hackathon, I deliberately kept my laptop off the big screen.

Normally, if there's a problem to solve and I think I can help, I want to get involved. Give me the keyboard.

This time I made a conscious effort not to. Other people took the lead, got stuck, tried things and worked it out together. I was involved, but I wasn't driving.
```

```text
A generated draft can be perfectly readable and still feel wrong.

I've noticed it while working on my blog. A small observation becomes a framework. A human detail gets another paragraph of explanation after it has already done the work. A sentence uses a perfectly good word I'd never say.

I've started recording some of those editorial decisions so the system has something more useful than an instruction to “write like me”.
```

```text
“Can you host and support this?” can sound like a small request once an application is working.

But hosting is only part of what the team is being asked to take on. There's the person who can't log in, the outage when the original developer is away, and the question of who decides what gets changed.

These are part of what it means to let people depend on something.

If we leave those questions until somebody asks where to host it, we're already quite late in the conversation.
```
