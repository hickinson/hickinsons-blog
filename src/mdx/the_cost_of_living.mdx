This is something I found myself thinking about over Christmas.

It started with a phrase we hear constantly: *the cost of living*. The more I sat with it, the more it felt like it was doing more than describing economics, it was shaping how we think about life itself.

  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    They call it the cost of living.<br />
    As if living were a service,<br />
    metered, itemised,<br />
    something that ticks quietly upward, while you sleep.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    A cost implies a bill.<br />
    A bill implies a collector.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    So who’s charging,<br />
    and why do the same hands,<br />
    always seem to reach deeper into the same pockets?
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    Because living, they say, has become expensive.<br />
    As though breath were a commodity,<br />
    warmth a luxury add-on,<br />
    time with your children an optional extra.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    But living isn’t costly.  
    Surviving is.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    Living is slow mornings, shared food.<br />
    Laughter that interrupts the plan.<br />
    Living is dignity without justification.<br />
    Living is not explaining yourself to a spreadsheet.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    Yet we’ve learned the phrase well.<br />
    We repeat it politely.<br />
    We nod as if it were weather,<br />
    unfortunate, unavoidable, nobody’s fault.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    Strange how the cost rises everywhere,<br />
    except where the profits sit.<br />
    Strange how belts tighten<br />
    but tables never get smaller at the top.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    And stranger still,<br />
    how this language travels.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    In one country it means heating or eating.<br />
    In another, it means time you’ll never get back.<br />
    Different currencies, same exchange rate:<br />
    life for money or money for life.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    At Christmas they’ll speak of resilience.<br />
    Of tough choices.<br />
    Of managing expectations.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    But no child measures wonder, in percentages.<br />
    No parent tucks in hope with a fiscal forecast.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    So maybe the question isn’t,<br />
    how much does it cost to live?
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    Maybe it’s how much living are we willing to lose<br />
    before we stop pretending this is normal.
  </p>

  <p className="mb-6">
    Because, maybe, living was never meant to be earned.<br />
    And if there is a price,<br />
    it shouldn’t be paid by those already giving everything.
